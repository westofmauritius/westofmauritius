When solar panels are connected to your home's electricity, they are also
connected to the Central Electricity Board (CEB) grid. That is why the CEB
must know about every system. This guide explains why registration matters
and what to ask your installer. For the exact, current procedure, always
check [ceb.mu](https://ceb.mu): the rules can change and only the CEB's own
pages are authoritative.

## Why registration matters

The CEB has warned that solar systems that are not registered may be
disconnected. An unregistered system can also put the people who work on the
grid at risk. Registering is not a formality you can skip.

## Selling surplus electricity

Electricity your panels make that you do not use can be sold back to the
CEB. How it is measured and paid depends on the scheme you join, so ask the
installer which scheme they will register your system under, and read its
conditions on [ceb.mu](https://ceb.mu).

## The CEB scheme for households

The CEB Solar Photovoltaic Scheme (Households) 2026 also covers hybrid
systems with a battery. If you are considering a battery, read our guide
[Battery or not?](/en/guides/battery-or-not).

## What to ask your installer

1. Will you handle the registration with the CEB, and is it included in the
   price?
2. Under which CEB scheme will the system be registered?
3. Who arranges any meter change, and is there a fee?
4. What documents do you need from me, and when?
5. When can the system be switched on: before or after the CEB has approved
   it?
