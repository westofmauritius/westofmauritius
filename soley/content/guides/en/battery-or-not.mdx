A home battery stores solar electricity so you can use it later: in the
evening, at night or during a power cut. It is the most common question
households ask us, and there is no single right answer. This guide helps you
decide what to ask installers.

## What a battery does, and what it does not

Solar panels produce electricity during the day, when many households use
the least. Without a battery, electricity you do not use straight away goes
to the CEB grid. With a battery, part of it is kept for later.

A battery does **not** make the panels produce more. It changes **when** you
use the electricity they produce.

## When a battery is worth considering

- **Most of your use is in the evening.** If the house is empty during the
  day and busy at night, a battery lets you use more of your own solar power.
- **Power cuts are a real problem for you.** A suitable battery system can
  keep essential circuits running. Ask the installer exactly which ones,
  because not every battery system works as backup power.
- **You want to depend less on the grid.**

## When it may not be worth it (yet)

- **You use most of your electricity during the day**, for example when you
  work from home or run appliances during the day.
- **Your budget is tight.** A battery adds a significant amount to the
  price. Panels alone usually pay back faster, and many systems can take a
  battery later. Ask the installer whether the inverter they propose is
  ready for one.

## What the official scheme says

The CEB Solar Photovoltaic Scheme (Households) 2026 also covers hybrid
systems with a battery, according to the CEB. Check the current conditions
on [ceb.mu](https://ceb.mu) before you decide, and ask installers whether the
system they quote qualifies for the government grant.

## Questions to ask every installer

1. What capacity (kWh) do you recommend, and why that size?
2. Which circuits will work during a power cut?
3. How long is the battery warranty, and what capacity is guaranteed at the
   end of it?
4. Can the battery be added later, and what would that cost?
5. Does the system qualify for the grant with the battery included?
